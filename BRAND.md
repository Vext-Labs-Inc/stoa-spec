## 1. The Vext naming dichotomy (corrected reading)

The current product family follows a clear pattern:

| Product | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Theron** | Mythological — Greek hunter | The 30-specialist council brain |
| **Sentry** | English martial noun | The v3 training stack |
| **Hive** | English biological noun | The Pro swarm |
| **AE OS** | Acronym + lowercase noun | The dashboard / OS layer |
| **Brain** | English anatomical noun | The ML / GNN / RL stack |
| **Vext** | Coined verb-ish | The parent brand |

The shape is **single-word, standalone, no hyphens, no acronyms in the name itself.** Mythological references are welcome (Theron). English martial / biological / structural nouns are welcome (Sentry, Hive, Brain). The product never carries the Vext prefix in its consumer-facing name — it stands alone, with "by Vext Labs" as the parent attribution.

My v1 candidates (Charter / Tessera / Vouch) were *standalone-protocol* names. They'd work for a public-foundation-stewarded standard. They do **not** fit the Vext family.

## 3. My pick: **STOA**

This is the right name. Five reasons:

1. **The metaphor is the product, exactly.** The Stoa was the colonnaded walkway in ancient Athens where philosophers and merchants worked under one roof — ideas became contracts, contracts became commerce. It was the literal substrate of the agora. Our product is the literal substrate of agent-readable SaaS. We don't have to invent a metaphor; we inherit the one that's been load-bearing for 2,500 years.
2. **It fits the family.** *Theron is the brain. Sentry is the training stack. Hive is the swarm. Stoa is the agent-readable SaaS standard.* That sentence reads like a coherent product family. "Charter is the standard" reads like a separate company.
3. **Greek + single-syllable + 4 letters.** Matches Theron's Greek lineage. Pronounceable in every English-speaking market on first read. Single-word, single-syllable, distinctive on a slide.
4. **Verb form works.** "We Stoa'd HubSpot last week." Awkward at first, brand-able with use — exactly like "we slacked it" was awkward in 2013.
5. **Less collision than any alternative.** Stoa School is online MBA prep — orthogonal audience, orthogonal category. No SaaS / agent / API competitor uses it. No procurement or engineering person has a competing Stoa in mind.

### 3.1 The taglines write themselves

> *"Stoa. The substrate of agent-readable SaaS. Built by Vext Labs."*
> *"Your software needs a surface agents can read. Stoa is that surface."*
> *"Stop training agents on your UI. Open the Stoa surface."*
> *"Stoa by Vext Labs — the agent-readable SaaS standard."*

### 3.2 The product family sentence

> *Vext Labs builds the OS for autonomous knowledge work. **Theron** is the brain — a council of 30 specialist 100B+ models. **Hive** is the Pro tier — an unbounded agent swarm built on Theron. **Sentry** is the training stack that makes the brain. **Stoa** is the protocol layer that lets the swarm reach into the rest of your software. **AE OS** is where you see all of it.*

That paragraph is the entire Vext brand in 5 sentences. Drops cleanly onto the home page.

## 5. Brand stance (assuming Stoa)

### 5.1 The one-line positioning

> *Stoa is the open standard that lets your SaaS speak machine. The colonnaded walkway under which your software and every agent on the planet can work together. Built by Vext Labs.*

### 5.2 The deeper positioning

Stoa exists because today's SaaS was built for humans pointing a mouse. Agents are forced through the same UI surface: scraping pixels, parsing rendered HTML, hallucinating against buttons that move. The result is the entire industry's current frustration with "unreliable agents."

Stoa lays a machine-readable surface alongside the human UI. A typed, contract-first, self-describing substrate designed for agents. The human UI keeps shipping; the Stoa contract is unchanged through any redesign. Your CRM keeps moving buttons; the agents keep working.

### 5.3 Brand voice (unchanged from v1)

- **Short sentences.** Annalea voice.
- **Concrete over abstract.**
- **Honest hedges.** Spec is open; we say what's not in it.
- **Vendor-first plural.** Speak to vendors as the protagonists; buyers are the audience.

### 5.4 Audiences (unchanged from v1)

1. SaaS founders / heads of platform — they publish the Stoa surface.
2. Engineering leaders deploying agents — they demand vendors open it.
3. Procurement / vendor-risk teams — Stoa conformance level becomes a vendor-risk attribute.
4. Press — they write the "the next API layer" story.

### 5.5 What Stoa is NOT

- **Not a Vext-only protocol.** Open standard. Vext is editor; spec hands off to a foundation at 5+ independently-conformant vendors.
- **Not a replacement for OpenAPI.** Sits *above* OpenAPI 3.1 + JSON Schema + MCP.
- **Not a Pro Hive feature.** Hive consumes Stoa natively, but Stoa the standard is open, free, vendor-facing.
- **Not a developer tool.** It's a vendor commitment.
- **Not proprietary infrastructure.** Spec, runtime, SDKs, conformance suite, registry — all open source under CC-BY-4.0 + Apache-2.0. Vext is editor of v0.1, not the long-term steward.

### 6.3 The 30-min vendor onboarding flow

Same 4-step path from v1, renamed:

1. **0–5 min:** Drop OpenAPI spec → Stoa generates a starter manifest at `<vendor>.stoa.dev/manifest.json`.
2. **5–15 min:** Annotate side-effects (idempotent / mutating / destructive) via wizard UI.
3. **15–25 min:** Run the conformance suite against staging.
4. **25–30 min:** Publish manifest URL → vendor listed at `core` level.

This lives at **stoa.dev/vendors/quick-start** and is the single conversion gate.

## 7. Sales channel + GTM motion

(All three motions from v1 carry over, renamed; the wedge mechanic and partner motion unchanged.)

### 7.1 The wedge — "the agent-traffic audit"

Every Pro Hive customer's audit log shows: *"X% of your CRM traffic this month came from agents, not humans."* Today 5–15%; by 2027 it crosses 50% for the average B2B SaaS. We weaponize this: Hive customers download an "Agent Traffic Audit" PDF and email it to their vendor with subject *"When are you publishing your Stoa surface?"*

Demand-side flywheel. Vendors get pinged by their own customers. Stoa becomes a vendor-side procurement requirement.

### 7.2 Three sales motions

| Motion | Audience | Channel | Pricing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard adoption** | SaaS founders / platform heads | Vendor docs, GitHub, conferences | Free |
| **Vext-attested adapter** | Vendors not ready to publish their own Stoa contract | Outbound to top-25; Vext builds + hosts + maintains the adapter | $2K/mo |
| **Marketplace traffic share** | Vendors who route Stoa calls through Vext billing | Self-serve onboarding | 12% of agent-traffic revenue |

### 7.3 Day-0 marketplace (target list — 25 vendors)

Same as v1 (top-25 SaaS by Pro Hive priority). All shipped as Vext-attested adapters for week-4 launch.

### 7.4 The press hook

Launch in week 4 of the FULL SEND plan, paired with Pro Hive P1 GA:

- **Anchor outlets:** TechCrunch + The Information — *"Vext launches Stoa, the open standard for agent-readable SaaS, with 25 vendors at launch."*
- **Founder essay:** Annalea on why the API era is ending. The colonnade visual is the cover image. Cross-posted to Stratechery + Vext blog.
- **The video.** A 90-second product film: an agent succeeds against a Stoa-surface SaaS, then fails against the same SaaS with only a human UI. Same task, two outcomes, one manifest difference (`{ "stoa": true }`).
- **Conference:** API-Days San Francisco (June 2026) launch keynote.

### 7.5 No agent-platform partner motion

We deliberately do not run partnership outreach to agent platforms (Cursor, Claude Desktop, Codeium, OpenAI Responses, Devin, Continue, Cline, Zed, Aider, etc.). The catalog is open, the gateway is free, and any MCP-capable client connects with one URL paste. We document the connect line; users find it themselves. See [POSITIONING.md §7](POSITIONING.md) for the full reasoning.

The other two partner motions stand:

1. **API gateways** (Kong, Tyk, Postman, Zuplo) — co-publish a Stoa generator that runs at the gateway, so any vendor sitting behind these gateways gets a Stoa contract for free. This is partner-friendly because the gateways' value prop is exactly "make APIs more consumable."
2. **Vendor-risk / procurement** (Vanta, Drata, SecureFrame) — add Stoa conformance level as a vendor-risk attribute alongside SOC 2. Procurement teams already use these tools; Stoa conformance becomes a checkbox in the buying workflow.
